# Getting Started

Bike's goal is to give you a better place to write, think, and organize your ideas. The core commands below are all you need. Bike has many other features, but you can pick those up later, or never. See [Use What You Need](#use-what-you-need).

## Core Commands

**To create a row:**

* Press the <kbd>Return</kbd> key to create a row

**To change a row's indentation level:**

* Press the <kbd>Tab</kbd> key to indent a row
* Press <kbd>Shift-Tab</kbd> key to unindent a row

**To move rows in the hierarchy:**

* Drag and drop rows by the row's triangle handle
* Press <kbd>Control-Command-Arrow</kbd> to move a row with children
* Press <kbd>Control-Option-Arrow</kbd> to move a row without children (like in a text editor)

**To show and hide detail in your outline:**

* Click a row's triangle handle to collapse the row (<kbd>Command-9</kbd>)
* Click that same handle to expand the row (<kbd>Command-0</kbd>)

**To focus into a specific part of your outline:**

* Click the focus button to the right of a row's text (<kbd>Option-Command-Right</kbd>)
* Click it again to unfocus an already focused row (<kbd>Option-Command-Left</kbd>)

**To filter your outline to show only matching rows:**

* Show and focus the filter bar (<kbd>Command-Shift-F</kbd>)
* Type a query to show matching rows

**To format text in your outline:**

* Show the formatting popover with <kbd>Command-E</kbd>
* Select formatting options from the popover with your mouse or with the single letter keyboard shortcuts displayed on the panel.

**To format rows in your outline:**

* In an empty row, type `#` followed by a space to make it a heading
* In an empty row, type `[]` followed by a space to make it a task
* More row types are there when you need them. See [Row Formatting](using-bike/row-formatting.md)

That's it, just a few commands and you've got a text editor with super powers.

## Use What You Need

The rest of this guide is optional.

Everything else is a secondary feature, built for a particular way of working, and designed to stay invisible until you need it.

Reaching for all of Bike's features at once works against the goal. Bike gets complex, and it stops being small enough to fully understand. I don't expect any one person to use all of it.

My suggestion: learn the commands above and do real work with them. Scan the rest of the guide to see what's possible, and then come back for the details when you feel like a feature could help your workflow.

Enjoy!
